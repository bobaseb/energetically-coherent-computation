\h{Theoretical Framework}

\begin{refsection}[references/0000_references.bib, references/0001_2_theoretical.bib]

The preceding chapters have established the philosophical foundations of Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC), exploring its departure from traditional computational theories of mind and situating it within broader debates in philosophy of consciousness. This chapter introduces the technical framework that transforms these philosophical commitments into a coherent scientific theory with explanatory and predictive power. Where traditional approaches have sought to understand consciousness through abstract computation or information processing, ECC grounds conscious experience in specific patterns of energy organization within biological neural systems—patterns that cannot be reduced to discrete symbolic operations or substrate-independent algorithms.

The theoretical framework presented here integrates insights from multiple disciplines—neuroscience, physics, molecular biology, and mathematics—to develop a comprehensive account of how consciousness emerges from coherent energy dynamics across multiple scales of organization. Beginning with core mechanisms that distinguish ECC from conventional computational approaches, we introduce the concept of neural light cones that define the spatial and temporal boundaries of conscious integration. We explore how synchronic and diachronic unity emerge from these dynamics, examine the role of rich alphabets shaped by transcriptomic profiles, and analyze how thermal noise establishes boundary conditions for conscious states. These foundations lead to a deeper investigation of energy's physical nature in conscious systems and the field dynamics that maintain coherence across neural networks. The framework culminates in a rigorous mathematical formalism—drawing on sheaf theory, tensor calculus, and recursive triangulation—that allows precise description of how local patterns of energetic coherence combine into the unified field we experience as consciousness. Throughout, we demonstrate how this framework resolves longstanding challenges in consciousness research while opening new avenues for empirical investigation and technological application.
