\section{\index{type and token identity theories}Type/Token Identity Theory}

The relationship between \index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)!relationship to identity theories}ECC and classical identity theories presents an intriguing synthesis that moves beyond traditional \index{type and token identity theories}type-type and token-token identity accounts of \index{consciousness}consciousness \cite{polger2009evaluating,armstrong2002materialist}. While \index{type and token identity theories!limitations of}type identity theory posits strict one-to-one correspondences between mental and physical states, and token identity theory allows for multiple physical realizations of the same mental state \cite{bechtel1999multiple}, ECC suggests a more nuanced position centered on patterns of \index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic coherence. This framework might be termed \index{coherence-class identity theory}coherence-class identity theory, where conscious states are identical with specific classes of energetically coherent physical states.

In traditional \index{type and token identity theories}type identity theory, each type of mental state is identical with a specific type of physical state \cite{place1956is,smart1959sensations}. \index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)}ECC modifies this view by suggesting that conscious states are identical not with specific physical configurations per se, but with patterns of \index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic coherence that might be realized through different but physically constrained \index{implementation!physical requirements for consciousness}implementations \cite{shapiro2000multiple,polger2016multiple}. Unlike token identity theory, which allows for arbitrary physical realizations, ECC argues that these implementations must support specific types of \index{energy dynamics}energy dynamics shaped by \index{transcriptomic profiles!and energetic coherence}transcriptomic profiles and molecular diversity.

This position maintains the \index{physicalism}physicalist commitments of identity theory while acknowledging that consciousness requires more than just the right physical structure—it requires the right kind of \index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic organization and coherence \cite{richardson2008multiple,difrisco2014hylomorphism}. The "types" in ECC's framework are defined not by specific physical configurations but by classes of energy dynamics that can support conscious experience. This allows for a limited form of \index{multiple realizability!in coherence-class identity theory}multiple realizability while still maintaining that consciousness is fundamentally a physical phenomenon \cite{kim1992multiple,mossio2015biological}.

This \index{coherence-class identity theory!contrast with traditional identity theories}coherence-class approach helps resolve several traditional problems faced by both type and token identity theories \cite{lewis1966argument,bennett2007mental}. Where classical type identity theory struggles to account for the apparent multiple realizability of mental states, and token identity theory risks making consciousness too abstract by allowing any suitable physical implementation, ECC's framework provides principled constraints on what kinds of physical systems could support consciousness. These constraints are based not on specific physical structures but on the capacity to maintain \index{coherence!across multiple scales}coherent energy dynamics across multiple scales \cite{wilson2001two,breakspear2005dynamics}.

The framework is particularly illuminating when considering the relationship between \index{brain structure!and conscious experience}brain structure and conscious experience \cite{block1972what,dehaene2011experimental}. Different brain regions with similar \index{transcriptomic profiles!role in conscious experience}transcriptomic profiles might achieve the same type of energetic coherence despite variations in their detailed physical structure. Conversely, regions with different profiles might support distinct types of conscious experience through their unique patterns of energy organization. This explains how the brain can maintain \index{stable states!role in consciousness}stable conscious states despite continuous molecular turnover and neural plasticity—it is the pattern of energetic coherence, rather than the specific \index{physical implementation!and consciousness}physical implementation, that remains constant \cite{polger2009evaluating,pinotsis2023vivo}.

This view also has important implications for understanding the \index{unity of consciousness!coherence-class account of}unity of consciousness \cite{bayne2010unity,mashour2020conscious}. Traditional identity theories struggle to explain how diverse physical states across the brain combine to create unified conscious experience. ECC suggests that unity emerges from the brain's capacity to maintain coherent energy dynamics across multiple regions, creating a unified field of consciousness through patterns of energetic organization rather than through identity with specific physical states. This coherent field allows for both the integration and differentiation that characterize conscious experience \cite{McFadden2020,hunt2024electromagnetic}.

\index{coherence-class identity theory!and phenomenal properties}ECC's coherence-class identity theory also provides new insights into the relationship between physical and \index{phenomenal properties!and coherence-class identity theory}phenomenal properties of consciousness \cite{chalmers2006strong}. Rather than attempting to identify \index{qualia!energetic basis of}qualia directly with physical states or treating them as emergent properties of information processing, this framework suggests that qualitative experiences are identical with specific patterns of energetic coherence \cite{varela1996neurophenomenology}. These patterns, shaped by transcriptomic profiles and molecular diversity, provide the \index{rich alphabet!of energetic states}rich alphabet necessary for the varied and nuanced character of conscious experience while maintaining its fundamentally physical nature.

This approach helps bridge the \index{explanatory gap!ECC's approach to}explanatory gap between physical and phenomenal properties without reducing one to the other \cite{lewis1966argument,chalmers1997conscious}. The qualitative aspects of consciousness are neither mysterious additions to physical reality nor mere computational abstractions, but rather are identical with particular classes of energetically coherent states. This preserves the physicalist foundations of identity theory while accounting for the distinctive \index{phenomenal properties!relationship to physical states}phenomenal character of conscious experience \cite{block1972what,seth2021being}.

The coherence-class framework also offers novel solutions to problems that have plagued traditional identity theories \cite{shapiro2000multiple,gillett2016reduction}. For instance, the issue of \index{multiple realizability!in coherence-class identity theory}multiple realizability, which has been a persistent challenge for type identity theory, takes on a different character when viewed through the lens of energetic coherence. While different physical implementations might support conscious experience, they must all achieve specific patterns of energetic organization—a constraint that provides a principled basis for limiting the scope of multiple realizability \cite{bechtel1999multiple,juarrero2023context}.

Moreover, this view helps explain why certain physical states give rise to particular phenomenal experiences \cite{kim1992multiple,da2024mathematical}. The connection between physical and experiential properties is not arbitrary but is grounded in the specific patterns of energetic coherence that different brain states can achieve. This helps explain both the regularity of conscious experience—why similar physical states reliably produce similar experiences—and its variability, as different patterns of energetic coherence can support different types of conscious states \cite{richardson2008multiple,bosl2025dynamical}.

The relationship between \index{local and global aspects!of consciousness}local and global aspects of consciousness also becomes clearer through this framework \cite{breakspear2005dynamics,van2025processes}. While traditional identity theories often struggle to explain how localized neural activity contributes to unified conscious experience, ECC's \index{local and global aspects!relationship in coherence-class identity theory}coherence-class approach shows how local patterns of energetic coherence can integrate into global conscious states through principled physical mechanisms. This integration is not merely additive but involves the maintenance of coherent energy dynamics across multiple scales \cite{pinotsis2023vivo,amil2024theta}.

This theoretical framework also has important implications for understanding the \index{temporal dynamics!of consciousness}temporal dynamics of consciousness \cite{zheng2025unbearable}. Rather than treating conscious states as static physical configurations, ECC emphasizes the importance of dynamic patterns of energetic coherence that unfold over time. This \index{temporal integration!in conscious experience}temporal aspect helps explain both the continuity of conscious experience and its capacity for rapid change, as patterns of energetic coherence can maintain stability while remaining responsive to new inputs and internal dynamics \cite{dehaene2011experimental,mashour2020conscious}.

The implications of coherence-class identity theory extend beyond theoretical concerns to practical questions about consciousness research and intervention \cite{hunt2024electromagnetic,butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights}. By identifying conscious states with specific patterns of energetic coherence, the framework suggests new approaches to measuring and manipulating consciousness. Rather than focusing solely on neural firing patterns or neurotransmitter levels, this view suggests that understanding consciousness requires tracking patterns of energetic organization across multiple scales \cite{Gidon2020,Beniaguev2021}.

This reconceptualization also has important implications for how we understand \index{disorders of consciousness!therapeutic approaches to}disorders of consciousness \cite{varela1996neurophenomenology,gallagher2020phenomenological}. Rather than viewing these conditions purely in terms of disrupted neural activity or chemical imbalances, ECC's framework suggests they might better be understood as perturbations in patterns of energetic coherence. This perspective could lead to new \index{therapeutic interventions!for consciousness disorders}therapeutic approaches that focus on restoring or maintaining appropriate patterns of energetic organization \cite{levin2020revisiting,McFadden2020}.

The \index{coherence-class identity theory}coherence-class identity perspective invites a deeper consideration of one of the most profound features of consciousness: its remarkable unity amid complexity \cite{bayne2010unity,mashour2020conscious}. If conscious states are identical with specific classes of energetically coherent physical states, how does the brain transform its vastly complex, high-dimensional patterns of activity into the seemingly singular, unified stream of experience we recognize as consciousness? \index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)!and unity of consciousness}ECC suggests that this transformation involves a fundamental process of \index{dimensionality reduction!in conscious experience}dimensionality reduction, whereby rich patterns of energetic coherence across distributed neural systems are integrated into a coherent, lower-dimensional field of conscious awareness \cite{da2024mathematical,thompson2010mind}. This process creates what appears to be a "\index{bottleneck of consciousness}bottleneck" in consciousness—a constrained yet remarkably flexible channel through which our diverse neural activity becomes unified experience \cite{dehaene2011experimental,van2025processes}. Understanding this process of integration and dimensionality reduction is essential for completing our account of how patterns of energetic coherence give rise to the unified field of consciousness that characterizes our moment-to-moment existence.
