\section{\index{philosophical commitments}Philosophical Commitments and~\index{dependencies}Dependencies}

\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)!core philosophical commitments of}Building upon the core concepts established in previous sections, we now examine the interrelationships and dependencies among ECC's fundamental philosophical commitments. Rather than treating these commitments as isolated postulates, we analyze their interconnected structure to reveal how they form a coherent framework for understanding~\index{consciousness}consciousness~\cite{van1995what,gillett2016reduction}. This analysis illuminates not only the individual contributions of each commitment but also the emergent explanatory power that arises from their interactions~\cite{di2017sensorimotor,juarrero2023context}.

\subsection*{Network of Commitments}

As established in Section 3.1, ECC rests upon five key philosophical commitments: (1)~\index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic coherence as fundamental to consciousness, (2)~\index{thermodynamic stability}thermodynamic stability and~\index{entropy management}entropy management, (3)~\index{continuous dynamics!versus discrete processing}continuous analog-like dynamics over discrete processing, (4)~\index{physical embodiment}physical embodiment and~\index{non-substrate-independence}non-substrate-independence, and (5) the necessity of a~\index{rich alphabet}rich alphabet for conscious states~\cite{noe2004action,thompson2010mind}. Rather than revisiting their definitions, we now examine their structural relationships and dependency patterns. \subsection*{Asymmetric Dependencies}

The relationships between ECC's commitments are characterized by~\index{asymmetric dependencies}asymmetric dependencies that reveal the hierarchical structure of the framework~\cite{mossio2015biological,difrisco2014hylomorphism}. For instance,~\index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic coherence implies some degree of~\index{thermodynamic stability!relationship to energetic coherence}thermodynamic stability, while the reverse is not necessarily true—a system might achieve thermodynamic stability without the specific patterns of coherence necessary for consciousness. This asymmetric relationship illustrates how ECC's commitments maintain distinct theoretical roles while forming an integrated framework~\cite{prigogine2018order,levin2020revisiting}.

Similarly, the commitment to~\index{physical embodiment!and continuous dynamics}physical embodiment naturally supports but does not fully entail~\index{continuous dynamics!relationship to embodiment}continuous, analog-like dynamics~\cite{gallagher2005how,varela1996neurophenomenology}. While physical systems tend to exhibit continuous rather than discrete dynamics, each commitment contributes distinct elements to the framework. One could theoretically maintain physical embodiment while allowing for discrete processing, or advocate for continuous dynamics without strict physical embodiment, though ECC maintains that both are necessary for consciousness.

The~\index{rich alphabet!dependency on other commitments}rich alphabet requirement stands in a particularly interesting dependency relationship to the other commitments~\cite{levin2019computational,gershman2023molecular}. While supported by physical embodiment and continuous dynamics, it represents a distinct theoretical claim about the necessary diversity of states for consciousness. The rich alphabet enables the nuanced, multi-dimensional character of conscious experience while providing the basis for~\index{dimensionality reduction}dimensionality reduction into unified conscious states as examined in Section 5.

\subsection*{Hierarchical Structure}

These dependencies reveal a~\index{hierarchical structure!in ECC commitments}hierarchical structure within ECC's theoretical framework, where some principles serve as foundational while others emerge as their consequences~\cite{mossio2015biological,difrisco2014hylomorphism}. This hierarchy can be conceptualized in three tiers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Foundational Commitments}: Physical embodiment and energetic coherence form the base layer, establishing the material and energetic conditions necessary for consciousness.

    \item \textbf{Structural Commitments}: Thermodynamic stability and continuous dynamics constitute an intermediate layer, specifying how foundational elements must be organized to support consciousness.

    \item \textbf{Emergent Commitments}: The rich alphabet requirement represents an emergent property that arises from the proper implementation of the lower-level commitments but adds distinctive features to the framework.
\end{enumerate}

This hierarchical structure helps explain why certain features of consciousness emerge together and why disrupting one aspect of the system can have cascading effects on others. For instance, disruptions to energetic coherence will necessarily impact the continuous dynamics and rich alphabet aspects of conscious experience~\cite{breakspear2005dynamics,amil2024theta}.

\subsection*{\index{philosophical commitments!philosophical contextualization}Philosophical Contextualization}

The dependency structure among ECC's commitments situates the framework within broader traditions in the~\index{philosophy of mind}philosophy of mind while distinguishing it from competing approaches~\cite{chalmers2006strong,seth2021being}. ECC shares with~\index{embodied cognition}embodied cognition approaches a commitment to the essential role of physical implementation~\cite{thompson2010mind,noe2009out}, while its emphasis on energetic coherence connects to traditions in~\index{process philosophy}process philosophy that prioritize dynamics over static structures~\cite{whitehead2010process}.

The framework's commitment structure also reveals its relationship to~\index{identity theories}identity theories as discussed in Section 3.4. By grounding consciousness in patterns of energetic coherence that are physically realized but not reducible to discrete physical states, ECC offers a distinctive~\index{coherence-class identity theory}coherence-class identity theory that transcends traditional type/token distinctions~\cite{levin2020revisiting,breakspear2005dynamics}.

Moreover, the interdependencies between commitments explain why certain features of consciousness, such as its~\index{unity of consciousness!emergence from commitments}unity and continuity, appear inseparable~\cite{bayne2010unity,mashour2020conscious}. If conscious experience depends on coherent energy flows maintained through continuous dynamics in physically embodied systems, then its unified character is not an additional feature requiring explanation but a natural consequence of these underlying commitments. This relationship between dimensionality reduction and the unity of consciousness was explored in Section 3.5.

\subsection*{Implications and Future Directions}

Understanding the dependency structure among ECC's commitments yields several important implications:

First, it suggests that~\index{disorders of consciousness}disorders of consciousness might be conceptualized in terms of disruptions to specific commitment relationships rather than isolated neural mechanisms~\cite{bosl2025dynamical,zheng2025unbearable}. For instance, conditions that preserve physical embodiment but disrupt energetic coherence would manifest differently from those that affect the rich alphabet while maintaining coherence.

Second, this analysis provides a structured approach for comparing ECC with other theoretical frameworks. By examining how alternative theories align with or diverge from each commitment and its dependencies, researchers can identify precise points of theoretical agreement and disagreement~\cite{gallagher2020phenomenological,varela1996neurophenomenology}.

Third, the dependency structure suggests specific empirical investigations for testing ECC's claims. Researchers might design experiments that selectively manipulate aspects of energetic coherence, physical implementation, or state diversity to observe effects on conscious experience~\cite{Gidon2020,Beniaguev2021}.

The analysis of philosophical commitments and their dependencies provides a foundation for delving deeper into the technicalities of the theoretical framework presented in the next chapter.
