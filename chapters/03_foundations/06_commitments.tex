\section{\index{philosophical commitments}Philosophical Commitments and~\index{dependencies}Dependencies}

\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)!core philosophical commitments of}ECC rests upon several fundamental~\index{philosophical commitments}philosophical commitments that, while distinct, form an interconnected framework for understanding~\index{consciousness}consciousness~\cite{van1995what,gillett2016reduction}. These commitments are not merely theoretical postulates but represent essential features of how consciousness emerges from~\index{physical implementation}physical systems. Understanding their~\index{interdependencies!between philosophical commitments}relationships and dependencies is crucial for evaluating ECC's explanatory power and identifying its core principles~\cite{di2017sensorimotor,juarrero2023context}.

The primary commitments of ECC can be organized into five key categories:\index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic coherence as fundamental to consciousness,~\index{thermodynamic stability}thermodynamic stability and~\index{entropy management}entropy management,~\index{continuous dynamics!versus discrete processing}continuous analog-like dynamics over discrete processing,~\index{physical embodiment}physical embodiment and~\index{non-substrate-independence}non-substrate-independence, and the necessity of a~\index{rich alphabet}rich alphabet for conscious states~\cite{noe2004action,thompson2010mind}. While these commitments are interrelated, they maintain degrees of independence that allow us to examine their individual contributions to the framework while acknowledging their interconnections.

\index{coherence}Energetic coherence, perhaps the most central commitment, posits that consciousness requires stable, organized~\index{energy flows}energy flows that maintain coherence across multiple scales~\cite{hunt2024electromagnetic,McFadden2020}. This commitment is closely linked to, but not entirely dependent on, the requirement for~\index{thermodynamic stability!relationship to energetic coherence}thermodynamic stability. While energetic coherence implies some degree of thermodynamic stability, the reverse is not necessarily true—a system might achieve thermodynamic stability without the specific patterns of coherence necessary for consciousness. This asymmetric~\index{dependencies!in ECC framework}dependency illustrates how ECC's commitments, while related, maintain distinct theoretical roles~\cite{prigogine2018order,levin2020revisiting}.

The commitment to~\index{continuous dynamics!in conscious systems}continuous,~\index{analog-like dynamics}analog-like dynamics and~\index{physical embodiment!and consciousness}physical embodiment represents another crucial relationship within ECC's theoretical structure~\cite{gallagher2005how,varela1996neurophenomenology}. While these commitments naturally align—physical systems tend to exhibit continuous rather than~\index{discrete state transitions}discrete dynamics—each contributes distinct elements to the framework. Physical embodiment ensures that conscious states are grounded in actual material systems, while the emphasis on continuous dynamics explains how these systems achieve the smooth, unified character of conscious experience~\cite{breakspear2005dynamics,van2025processes}. However, neither commitment fully entails the other; one could theoretically maintain physical embodiment while allowing for discrete processing, or advocate for continuous dynamics without strict physical embodiment.

The~\index{rich alphabet!relationship to other ECC commitments}rich alphabet requirement stands in a particularly interesting relationship to the other commitments~\cite{levin2019computational,gershman2023molecular}. This commitment holds that consciousness requires a diverse range of possible states, shaped by~\index{transcriptomic profiles}transcriptomic profiles and molecular diversity, rather than the limited alphabet of binary or digital systems. While this commitment is supported by physical embodiment and continuous dynamics, it represents a distinct theoretical claim about the nature of conscious states~\cite{pinotsis2023vivo,amil2024theta}. The rich alphabet enables the nuanced, multi-dimensional character of conscious experience while providing the basis for~\index{dimensionality reduction}dimensionality reduction into unified conscious states.

These relationships reveal a~\index{hierarchical structure!in ECC commitments}hierarchical structure within ECC's commitments, where some principles serve as foundational supports for others~\cite{mossio2015biological,difrisco2014hylomorphism}. For instance, physical embodiment and energetic coherence provide the basis for continuous dynamics and the rich alphabet, while thermodynamic stability acts as a constraint on how these features can be realized in actual systems. This hierarchy helps explain why certain features of consciousness emerge together and why disrupting one aspect of the system can have cascading effects on others.

Understanding these dependencies also helps clarify ECC's position on broader questions in~\index{philosophy of mind}philosophy of mind~\cite{chalmers2006strong,seth2021being}. For instance, the framework's commitment to physical embodiment and energetic coherence explains its skepticism toward~\index{computational theories of mind}computational theories of consciousness. While computation might play a role in organizing and structuring conscious experience, ECC suggests that computation alone—divorced from specific physical implementations and energy dynamics—cannot give rise to consciousness~\cite{piccinini2013neural,anderson2024physical}. This position emerges naturally from the interplay of ECC's core commitments rather than being an additional theoretical assumption.

The framework's commitments also illuminate why certain features of consciousness, such as its~\index{unity of consciousness!relationship to philosophical commitments}unity and continuity, appear to be inseparable~\cite{bayne2010unity,mashour2020conscious}. If conscious experience depends on coherent energy flows maintained through continuous dynamics in physically embodied systems, then its unified character is not an additional feature requiring explanation but a natural consequence of these underlying commitments. Similarly, the rich alphabet requirement helps explain why conscious experience exhibits such nuance and complexity while remaining coherent~\cite{da2024mathematical,van2025processes}.

These~\index{philosophical commitments!dependencies between}philosophical commitments and their dependencies point toward a fundamental critique of traditional computationalist approaches to consciousness~\cite{piccinini2013neural,anderson2024physical}. While computationalism has dominated cognitive science and~\index{artificial intelligence}artificial intelligence research, ECC's framework suggests that this dominance may have led us astray in our understanding of consciousness. The limitations of computational approaches become particularly clear when we examine how they fail to account for the physical and energetic requirements that ECC identifies as essential to conscious experience~\cite{butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights,seth2024conscious}.

The relationship between these commitments also helps explain why certain approaches to~\index{artificial consciousness}artificial consciousness may be fundamentally misguided~\cite{hunt2024electromagnetic,McFadden2020}. If consciousness requires specific forms of energetic coherence maintained through physical embodiment, then attempts to create conscious machines through purely computational means are unlikely to succeed. This suggests that the development of artificial consciousness might require fundamentally different approaches that prioritize the physical implementation of coherent energy dynamics~\cite{levin2020revisiting,pinotsis2023vivo}.

Moreover, the~\index{interdependencies!in conscious systems}interdependencies between ECC's commitments help explain why consciousness appears to be an all-or-nothing phenomenon in certain respects while admitting of degrees in others~\cite{dehaene2011experimental,mashour2020conscious}. The requirement for coherent energy flows across multiple scales creates natural thresholds that must be met for consciousness to emerge, while the rich alphabet of possible states allows for variation in the quality and complexity of conscious experience once these thresholds are achieved~\cite{breakspear2005dynamics,amil2024theta}.

The framework's emphasis on physical embodiment and energetic coherence also provides new perspectives on the relationship between consciousness and~\index{living systems!relationship to consciousness}life~\cite{mossio2015biological,difrisco2014hylomorphism}. The commitments suggest that consciousness might be more closely tied to fundamental biological processes than traditional computational approaches would indicate, while still maintaining that not all living systems necessarily give rise to conscious experience~\cite{levin2019computational,gershman2023molecular}.

The interaction between ECC's commitments and their implications for understanding consciousness suggests new directions for both theoretical and empirical research~\cite{varela1996neurophenomenology,gallagher2020phenomenological}. By identifying the essential requirements for consciousness and their interdependencies, the framework provides guidance for developing experimental protocols and interpreting empirical results. This helps bridge the gap between philosophical analysis and scientific investigation~\cite{chalmers2006strong,juarrero2023context}.

These theoretical commitments also have important implications for understanding~\index{disorders of consciousness}disorders of consciousness and potential~\index{therapeutic interventions}therapeutic interventions~\cite{bosl2025dynamical,zheng2025unbearable}. The framework suggests that treating such disorders requires attention not just to individual neural mechanisms but to the broader patterns of energetic coherence that support conscious experience. This multilevel approach emerges naturally from the interdependencies between ECC's core commitments~\cite{Gidon2020,Beniaguev2021}.

The analysis of these philosophical commitments naturally leads to a systematic critique of~\index{computational theories of mind!critique of}computationalist approaches to consciousness~\cite{piccinini2013neural,anderson2024physical}. While acknowledging the importance of information processing in neural systems, ECC's framework reveals fundamental limitations in attempting to reduce consciousness to computation alone.

The~\index{philosophical commitments!contrast with computationalism}philosophical commitments articulated above—\index{coherence!energetic basis of}energetic coherence,~\index{thermodynamic stability}thermodynamic stability,~\index{continuous dynamics!versus discrete processing}continuous dynamics,~\index{physical embodiment}physical embodiment, and a~\index{rich alphabet!necessity for consciousness}rich state alphabet—collectively establish a framework that stands in direct contrast to traditional~\index{computationalism!assumptions of}computationalist assumptions~\cite{piccinini2020neurocognitive,dreyfus1992computers}. Where computationalism posits consciousness as~\index{substrate independence!in computationalism}substrate-independent information processing implementable across various physical systems, ECC's commitments reveal why such an abstraction fundamentally mischaracterizes the nature of conscious experience~\cite{horst2011symbols,butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights}. This contrast is not merely a difference in emphasis but reflects incompatible views regarding consciousness's essential properties. The dominance of the~\index{computational paradigm!historical dominance of}computational paradigm in cognitive science has yielded important insights into information processing aspects of cognition, yet its fundamental limitations become apparent when confronting the embodied, energetic, and continuous nature of conscious experience~\cite{thompson2010mind,noe2009out}. A thorough~\index{computational theories of mind!systematic critique of}examination of these limitations not only clarifies ECC's distinctive contribution but also illustrates why conventional computational approaches—despite their mathematical elegance and apparent explanatory power—cannot adequately account for the fundamental properties of consciousness that emerge from the interdependent commitments described in this section~\cite{searle1980minds,van1995might}. The following critical analysis will demonstrate how the~\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)!critique of computational paradigm}ECC framework provides resources for understanding precisely where and why computational approaches fall short in explaining consciousness.
