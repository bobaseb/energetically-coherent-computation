This process creates what we experience as the "\index{bottleneck of consciousness}bottleneck" of consciousness—the seemingly singular stream of awareness that characterizes our moment-to-moment experience.

Unlike computational approaches that view dimensionality reduction as purely information processing, ECC suggests that this reduction is fundamentally tied to the brain's capacity to maintain coherent \index{energy flows}energy flows \cite{dehaene2011experimental,breakspear2005dynamics}. The process begins with the \index{rich alphabet}rich, high-dimensional alphabet of possible states shaped by \index{transcriptomic profiles}transcriptomic profiles across different brain regions. These states represent the full complexity of neural activity, including sensory inputs, \index{memory}memories, emotions, and cognitive processes \cite{bayne2010unity,gershman2023molecular}. Through the maintenance of specific patterns of energetic coherence, this complexity is transformed into a lower-dimensional field that supports unified \index{conscious experience}conscious experience.

This reduction is not simply a matter of filtering or selecting information; rather, it involves the active organization of \index{energy flows!in dimensionality reduction}energy flows into \index{stable states}stable, coherent patterns that can support conscious awareness \cite{mashour2020conscious,pinotsis2023vivo}. The process is inherently \index{dynamic stability}dynamic, with the brain continuously adjusting its patterns of energetic coherence to maintain a unified field of consciousness while responding to changing internal and external demands. This explains why consciousness feels both unified and dynamic—it represents a continuously updated reduction of high-dimensional neural activity into a coherent, lower-dimensional field \cite{carhart2014entropic,amil2024theta}.

The concept of a \index{bottleneck of consciousness!contrast with information processing bottlenecks}conscious bottleneck in ECC differs fundamentally from traditional information processing bottlenecks. Rather than representing a limitation in computational capacity, this bottleneck reflects the brain's active organization of energetic coherence into unified conscious states \cite{bayne2003what,van2025processes}. The reduction in dimensionality serves several crucial functions: it enables stable conscious experiences, facilitates \index{decision-making!role of dimensionality reduction in}decision-making, and allows for the integration of diverse neural processes into a coherent \index{stream of consciousness}stream of awareness \cite{dainton2006stream,varela1996neurophenomenology}.

This process of dimensionality reduction is intimately tied to the brain's \index{thermodynamic constraints}thermodynamic constraints \cite{hameroff2014consciousness,prigogine2018order}. Maintaining coherent, \index{low-entropy states}low-entropy states across \index{neural networks}neural networks requires significant energy expenditure, making it inefficient to sustain high-dimensional conscious states. The reduction to a lower-dimensional field represents an optimal solution, allowing the brain to achieve stable, unified consciousness while managing its energetic resources effectively \cite{Berndt2012,pellerin2004neuroenergetics}. This explains why consciousness appears to have a limited capacity—it reflects the brain's need to balance the maintenance of coherent states with \index{thermodynamic constraints!and dimensionality reduction}thermodynamic efficiency.

This creates a natural constraint on the dimensionality reduction process, helping to explain why conscious experience appears both unified and bounded.

The dimensionality reduction framework also helps explain the \index{temporal dynamics}temporal dynamics of conscious experience \cite{varela1999present,amil2024theta}. As the brain processes new inputs and generates new patterns of neural activity, the reduction process continuously updates the unified field of consciousness. This creates the seamless flow of conscious experience we observe, where each moment smoothly transitions into the next while maintaining coherence \cite{zheng2025unbearable,van2025processes}. The process is not merely sequential but involves continuous \index{feedback systems!between higher and lower dimensional states}feedback between higher and lower dimensional states, allowing consciousness to remain both stable and responsive to change.

Importantly, this view of unity and dimensionality reduction has implications for understanding both normal consciousness and \index{altered states of consciousness}altered states \cite{carhart2014entropic,dehaene2011experimental}. This might manifest as fragmented awareness, altered states of consciousness, or even complete loss of consciousness when the brain cannot maintain the necessary patterns of energetic coherence \cite{mashour2020conscious,pinotsis2023vivo}.
