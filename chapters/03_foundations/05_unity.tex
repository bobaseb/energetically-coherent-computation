\section{\index{unity of consciousness}Unity of~\index{consciousness}Consciousness and~\index{dimensionality reduction}Dimensionality Reduction}

Building on the coherence-class identity theory established in Section 4, this section examines how~\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)}ECC explains one of consciousness's most fundamental properties: its remarkable unity amid neural complexity. Rather than treating unity as merely an emergent property, ECC positions~\index{dimensionality reduction}dimensionality reduction as a core explanatory mechanism through which consciousness achieves its unified character.

\subsection{Dimensionality Reduction as a Fundamental Process}

Consciousness emerges through a process whereby complex, high-dimensional patterns of energetic coherence are transformed into a lower-dimensional, unified field of experience~\cite{da2024mathematical,zheng2025unbearable}. This process creates what we experience as the "\index{bottleneck of consciousness}bottleneck" of consciousness—the seemingly singular stream of awareness that characterizes our moment-to-moment experience~\cite{tononi2016integrated,bayne2010unity}.

Unlike computational approaches that view dimensionality reduction as purely information processing, ECC suggests that this reduction is fundamentally tied to the brain's capacity to maintain coherent~\index{energy flows}energy flows~\cite{dehaene2011experimental,breakspear2005dynamics}. The process begins with high-dimensional neural states shaped by the transcriptomic profiles established in Section 1. Through the maintenance of specific patterns of energetic coherence, this complexity is transformed into a lower-dimensional field that supports unified~\index{conscious experience}conscious experience.

This reduction is not simply filtering information; rather, it involves the active organization of energy flows into stable, coherent patterns that can support conscious awareness~\cite{mashour2020conscious,pinotsis2023vivo}. The process is inherently~\index{dynamic stability}dynamic, with the brain continuously adjusting its patterns of energetic coherence to maintain a unified field of consciousness while responding to changing internal and external demands~\cite{carhart2014entropic,amil2024theta}.

\subsection{The Neural Light Cone and Causal Constraints}

The concept of the~\index{neural light cones}neural light cone provides crucial constraints on the dimensionality reduction process~\cite{da2024mathematical,bosl2025dynamical}. Formally, the neural light cone $L(x,t)$ defines the spatiotemporal region that can causally influence or be influenced by neural activity at position $x$ and time $t$, constrained by the maximum propagation speed $v$ of relevant neural signals:

\begin{equation}
L(x,t) = \{(y,s) \in \mathbb{R}^3 \times \mathbb{R} : |y-x| \leq v|s-t|\}
\end{equation}

As conscious experience is reduced to a lower-dimensional field, the neural light cone defines the~\index{neural light cones!boundaries of conscious experience}boundaries within which this reduction can maintain~\index{neural light cones!and causal coherence}causal coherence. Information outside the light cone cannot contribute to the current conscious state, ensuring that consciousness remains causally unified despite its distributed physical basis~\cite{hunt2024electromagnetic,McFadden2020}.

The light cone concept unifies both spatial and temporal aspects of conscious integration. Spatially, it explains why only certain brain regions contribute to consciousness at any moment. Temporally, it explains why consciousness exhibits a particular integration window (typically ~100-200ms), beyond which events cannot be consciously bound together~\cite{varela1999present,mashour2020conscious}.

This causal structure creates natural constraints on the dimensionality reduction process, helping to explain why conscious experience appears both unified and bounded. Neural activity patterns that fall outside an individual's neural light cone cannot be integrated into the current conscious state, regardless of their informational relevance~\cite{da2024mathematical,bosl2025dynamical}.

\subsection{Energetic Basis of Dimensionality Reduction}

The dimensionality reduction process is intimately tied to the brain's~\index{thermodynamic constraints}thermodynamic constraints~\cite{hameroff2014consciousness,prigogine2018order}. Maintaining coherent,~\index{low-entropy states}low-entropy states across~\index{neural networks}neural networks requires significant energy expenditure, making it thermodynamically inefficient to sustain high-dimensional conscious states.

The reduction to a lower-dimensional field represents an optimal solution, allowing the brain to achieve stable, unified consciousness while managing its energetic resources effectively~\cite{Berndt2012,pellerin2004neuroenergetics}. This explains why consciousness appears to have a limited capacity—it reflects the brain's need to balance the maintenance of coherent states with~\index{thermodynamic constraints!and dimensionality reduction}thermodynamic efficiency.

The~\index{concept of bottleneck!energetic vs. computational}concept of a conscious bottleneck in ECC differs fundamentally from traditional information processing bottlenecks. Rather than representing a limitation in computational capacity, this bottleneck reflects the brain's active organization of energetic coherence into unified conscious states~\cite{bayne2003what,van2025processes}. The reduction in dimensionality serves several crucial functions: it enables stable conscious experiences, facilitates~\index{decision-making!role of dimensionality reduction in}decision-making, and allows for the integration of diverse neural processes into a coherent~\index{stream of consciousness}stream of awareness~\cite{dainton2006stream,varela1996neurophenomenology}.

\subsection{Mathematical Framework for Dimensionality Reduction}

The dimensionality reduction process can be formalized mathematically as a mapping function $f: \mathbb{R}^n \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^m$ where $n \gg m$, transforming the high-dimensional neural state space to the lower-dimensional conscious state space~\cite{da2024mathematical,thompson2010mind}. Crucially, this mapping is constrained by energetic coherence requirements, such that:

\begin{equation}
f(S) = C \text{ where } C \in \mathbb{R}^m \text{ and } E(C) \geq \tau
\end{equation}

Here, $S$ represents the high-dimensional neural state, $C$ the conscious state, $E$ a measure of energetic coherence, and $\tau$ a threshold value below which conscious states cannot be maintained.

This formalism captures several key insights: (1) consciousness represents a dramatic reduction in dimensionality from neural to phenomenal states, (2) this reduction is constrained by energetic coherence requirements, and (3) the mapping preserves specific relational structures within the high-dimensional space while discarding others~\cite{carhart2014entropic,bosl2025dynamical}.

The coherence measure $E$ can be further decomposed into components representing local coherence within neural subsystems and global coherence across subsystems:

\begin{equation}
E(C) = \alpha E_{local}(C) + \beta E_{global}(C)
\end{equation}

where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are weighting parameters that may vary across brain states and individuals. This formalization allows us to model how disruptions to either local or global coherence might affect the dimensionality reduction process and, consequently, conscious experience~\cite{breakspear2005dynamics,mashour2020conscious}.

\subsection{Temporal Dynamics of Unified Consciousness}

The dimensionality reduction framework also illuminates the~\index{temporal dynamics}temporal dynamics of conscious experience~\cite{varela1999present,amil2024theta}. As the brain processes new inputs and generates new patterns of neural activity, the reduction process continuously updates the unified field of consciousness. This creates the seamless flow of conscious experience we observe, where each moment smoothly transitions into the next while maintaining coherence~\cite{zheng2025unbearable,van2025processes}.

The process is not merely sequential but involves continuous~\index{feedback systems!between higher and lower dimensional states}feedback between higher and lower dimensional states, allowing consciousness to remain both stable and responsive to change. This feedback system creates what has been called the "dynamic core" of consciousness—a continuously evolving but coherent pattern of activity that maintains unity across time~\cite{tononi2016integrated,bayne2010unity}.

This view also helps explain both normal consciousness and~\index{altered states of consciousness}altered states~\cite{carhart2014entropic,dehaene2011experimental}. Disruptions to the brain's capacity for coherent energy organization can affect the dimensionality reduction process, leading to changes in conscious experience. This might manifest as fragmented awareness, altered states of consciousness, or even complete loss of consciousness when the brain cannot maintain the necessary patterns of energetic coherence~\cite{mashour2020conscious,pinotsis2023vivo}.

The~\index{astrocytic networks!role in dimensionality reduction}astrocytic networks introduced in Section 1 play a crucial role in this temporal integration. These networks provide the infrastructure necessary for maintaining coherent energy states across different brain regions and across time, helping to explain how the brain achieves temporal continuity in conscious experience. The continuous,~\index{field-like properties}field-like properties of astrocytic networks support the smooth reduction of high-dimensional neural activity into unified conscious states that persist through time~\cite{hunt2024electromagnetic,McFadden2020}.

\subsection{Implications for the Study of Consciousness}

The dimensionality reduction framework has significant implications for both theoretical models and empirical investigations of consciousness~\cite{seth2021being,butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights}. It suggests that measuring consciousness requires tracking not just neural activity patterns but the organization and reduction of energetic coherence across multiple scales and over time.

The relationship between~\index{conscious and unconscious processing!in dimensionality reduction framework}conscious and unconscious processing also takes on new significance through this lens~\cite{dehaene2011experimental,mashour2020conscious}. Rather than viewing unconscious processes as simply lacking some critical property, ECC suggests that they represent neural activity that has not been integrated into the reduced dimensional space of conscious experience. This helps explain phenomena like~\index{subliminal perception!in dimensionality reduction framework}subliminal perception and implicit learning while maintaining the fundamental distinction between conscious and unconscious processing~\cite{van2025processes,amil2024theta}.

This understanding of unity through dimensionality reduction reveals how deeply consciousness depends on particular forms of physical organization and energetic dynamics. The capacity to transform complex, high-dimensional patterns into a unified field of experience is not incidental to consciousness but constitutes its very essence~\cite{bayne2010unity,thompson2010mind}.

This framework naturally leads to questions about the philosophical commitments that underpin the distinctive properties of conscious systems.
