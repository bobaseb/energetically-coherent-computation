The relationship between physical implementation~\index{physical implementation} and computational representation takes on particular significance in CPS design~\cite{Tao2019,Wheeler2005}. Unlike purely computational systems, CPS must maintain coherence across both physical and digital domains while supporting real-time interaction and adaptation. The framework provides new perspectives on how digital twins and CPS might support conscious-like processing through sophisticated management of physical-digital interactions~\index{physical-computational integration}. Such synthesis could provide practical paths toward developing artificial systems capable of supporting genuine conscious-like processing through coherent physical-digital interaction~\cite{White2021,hunt2024electromagnetic}.

Digital twins and cyber-physical systems represent sophisticated approaches to bridging physical and computational domains while maintaining coherent energy dynamics, yet they operate fundamentally within classical physics. This raises an intriguing question: might computational paradigms that exploit non-classical physics offer new insights into conscious-like processing? Quantum computing emerges as a natural subject for examination, as quantum systems inherently operate through continuous, wave-like processes that ostensibly align with ECC's emphasis on field-like properties and coherent dynamics. At first glance, quantum computing seems promising for consciousness—quantum systems maintain coherent superpositions of states, process information through interference patterns, and demonstrate non-local effects that appear conceptually aligned with the unified yet distributed nature of conscious experience. However, closer examination reveals profound distinctions that illuminate key aspects of ECC's framework and the physical requirements for consciousness. These distinctions help clarify what energetic coherence actually entails and why quantum approaches, despite their computational power, may not directly advance artificial consciousness.
