\section{What is Computation?}

The classical theory of computation, developed through pioneering work on effective procedures and mechanical calculation, established computation as the manipulation of\index{discrete symbolic operations}discrete symbols according to formal rules~\cite{turing1936}. However, ECC suggests that\index{consciousness}consciousness requires a fundamentally different kind of\index{computation}computation—one grounded in\index{continuous dynamics}continuous,\index{physical embodiment}physically embodied\index{energy flows}energy flows rather than\index{symbolic manipulation}abstract symbol manipulation~\cite{da2024mathematical}.

Traditional\index{computational paradigm}computational theory centers on the notion that any computation can be reduced to a sequence of simple, mechanical steps executed by an\index{Turing machine}abstract machine~\cite{landauer1996}. The\index{Turing machine!universal}universal Turing machine demonstrated that all\index{classical computation}classical computation could be realized through the manipulation of discrete symbols on an infinite tape. The\index{Church-Turing thesis}Church-Turing thesis suggested that any intuitively computable function could be computed by these equivalent formalisms, establishing a foundational principle for computer science~\cite{butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights}.

However, these classical approaches share crucial assumptions that\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)}ECC challenges. First, they presume that information can be perfectly encoded in\index{discrete state transitions}discrete states, while ECC argues that\index{conscious processing}conscious processing requires\index{analog-like dynamics}continuous, analog-like states that cannot be \textbf{fully} captured by discrete representations~\cite{pinotsis2023cytoelectric}. Second, the Church-Turing thesis implies that computation is independent of its\index{physical implementation}physical implementation, whereas ECC contends that consciousness requires specific\index{physical properties}physical properties and\index{energy dynamics}energy dynamics that cannot be abstracted away~\cite{hunt2024electromagnetic}.

These theoretical tensions highlight fundamental questions about the nature of computation itself. While the Church-Turing thesis may capture the essence of abstract symbol manipulation, consciousness appears to require what we might term\index{natural computation}natural computation—\index{computation!as physically embodied processes}physically embodied processes that cannot be reduced to\index{discrete symbolic operations}discrete symbolic operations. This suggests that understanding consciousness requires expanding our conception of computation beyond traditional algorithmic frameworks.

This perspective aligns with emerging understanding of how\index{biological systems}biological systems process information through continuous, physically grounded dynamics rather than discrete state transitions.

Recent theoretical work has begun to challenge the assumption that all natural processes admit computational description~\cite{van2025processes}. Just as digestion cannot be adequately characterized as information processing, and gravitational phenomena cannot be reduced to computation,\index{conscious experience}conscious experience may emerge from\index{physical dynamics}physical dynamics that resist computational abstraction~\cite{zheng2025unbearable}. This aligns with growing skepticism about the\index{computational theory of mind}computational theory of mind, particularly regarding the context-sensitivity and holistic nature of conscious thought.

The framework suggests that attempting to reduce consciousness to computation represents a fundamental\index{category error!in computational theories of mind}category error—confusing the abstract map of computational description with the physical territory of conscious experience. While computational models may capture certain aspects of cognitive processing, they fundamentally miss the\index{field-like properties}continuous, field-like properties that characterize conscious experience~\cite{bosl2025dynamical}. This limitation becomes particularly evident when considering how consciousness maintains coherence across distributed neural processes.

Traditional\index{computationalism}computationalism faces particular challenges in explaining the\index{temporal dynamics!of consciousness}temporal dynamics of consciousness~\cite{amil2024theta}. The continuous flow of conscious experience appears fundamentally at odds with the\index{discrete state transitions}discrete state transitions that characterize classical computation. ECC suggests that this\index{temporal continuity}temporal continuity emerges naturally from the physical dynamics of coherent energy flows, rather than requiring additional computational mechanisms to bridge discrete states.

The implications extend beyond theoretical understanding to practical questions about\index{artificial consciousness}artificial consciousness~\cite{butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights}. While digital computers excel at manipulating discrete symbols according to formal rules, they may be fundamentally incapable of supporting the specific forms of energetic coherence that ECC identifies as essential to conscious experience. This suggests that creating conscious artificial systems might require radically different approaches to computation and physical implementation.

The distinction between\index{classical computation!contrast with conscious processing}classical computation and\index{conscious processing}conscious processing becomes particularly evident when examining how biological systems maintain\index{coherent states}coherent states across multiple scales~\cite{breakspear2005dynamics}. Rather than viewing\index{neural computation}neural computation as analogous to digital processing, ECC proposes that conscious systems compute through patterns of energetic coherence that enable both stability and flexibility. The relationship between\index{energy}energy and\index{information processing}information takes on particular significance in this context~\cite{attwell2001}. The framework also provides insight into why certain aspects of conscious experience appear resistant to computational modeling~\cite{demetrius2015}. Features such as\index{qualitative aspects!of consciousness}qualitative experience,\index{temporal continuity}temporal continuity, and\index{global integration}global integration may emerge naturally from the physical dynamics of conscious systems while remaining fundamentally irreducible to discrete computational processes. The framework thus suggests a fundamental revision in how we understand the relationship between computation and consciousness~\cite{hunt2024electromagnetic}. Rather than treating consciousness as an emergent property of abstract computation, ECC positions it as arising from specific forms of physical computation that maintain coherent energy dynamics across multiple scales. This perspective provides new insights into both the possibilities and limitations of conscious systems while suggesting productive directions for future research and development~\cite{da2024mathematical}.
