\section{IIT and Panpsychism}

The relationship between~\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)!relationship to IIT}\index{Integrated Information Theory (IIT)!comparison with ECC}\index{panpsychism!comparison with ECC}Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC), Integrated Information Theory (IIT), and panpsychism reveals important theoretical convergences while maintaining distinct positions on consciousness's physical basis. Where IIT posits consciousness as identical to~\index{information integration!IIT's perspective on}integrated information~\cite{Tononi2008, Tononi2016}, and~\index{panpsychism!consciousness as fundamental in}panpsychism suggests consciousness pervades all matter~\cite{Strawson2006, Goff2019}, ECC frames consciousness as emerging from specific patterns of~\index{energetic coherence!forms required for consciousness}energetic coherence within biological systems.

IIT offers a mathematical framework for quantifying consciousness through~\index{Integrated Information Theory (IIT)!Phi measure in}integrated information ($\Phi$)~\cite{Tononi2016, Oizumi2014}. While ECC appreciates IIT's attempt to provide rigorous measures, it suggests that integration emerges from physical energy dynamics rather than abstract information processing. This distinction is crucial - where~\index{Integrated Information Theory (IIT)!substrate independence in}\index{substrate independence!in IIT}IIT remains largely substrate-independent, ECC insists on specific physical requirements through energetic coherence. The~\index{rich alphabet!transcriptomic basis of}rich alphabet requirement in ECC, shaped by transcriptomic profiles and cellular organization, provides concrete constraints on possible implementations of consciousness beyond IIT's more abstract specifications.

ECC aligns with panpsychism in rejecting purely computational approaches to consciousness and emphasizing its grounding in physical reality~\cite{Nagel1979, Chalmers2015}. However, where~\index{panpsychism!contrast with ECC}panpsychism attributes consciousness to all matter, ECC suggests consciousness requires specific forms of energetic coherence typically found only in biological systems. This more restricted view helps avoid the~\index{panpsychism!combination problem in}combination problem that plagues panpsychist accounts~\cite{Chalmers2015, Skrbina2017} - how simple forms of consciousness combine into complex experiences. ECC explains this through coherent energy dynamics and field integration.

The theories also differ in their treatment of~\index{emergent properties!of consciousness}emergence~\cite{Goff2019, Shani2015}. Panpsychism often posits consciousness as fundamental, while IIT suggests it emerges from information integration~\cite{Oizumi2014, Tononi2021}. ECC charts a middle path, proposing that consciousness emerges from specific patterns of energetic coherence while remaining irreducibly physical. This approach maintains connection to physical reality while explaining why consciousness appears only in certain types of organized systems.

ECC addresses the explanatory gap between physical processes and~\index{phenomenal experience!energetic basis of}conscious experience~\cite{Koch2019, Chalmers2018} by suggesting how specific patterns of energetic coherence give rise to conscious experiences through their inherent physical dynamics. This provides a more concrete basis for understanding both the emergence of consciousness and its qualitative features, without making consciousness ubiquitous like panpsychism or reducing it to information like IIT.

The~\index{dynamic core hypothesis!bridging neural integration theories}dynamic core hypothesis~\cite{Baars2013, Mashour2020} provides an important historical bridge between neural integration theories and ECC's framework. Where the dynamic core hypothesis proposed that consciousness emerges from integrated neural activity in~\index{dynamic core hypothesis!thalamocortical systems in}thalamocortical systems, ECC extends this insight by specifying how such integration occurs through coherent energy dynamics. Both approaches emphasize dynamic integration~\cite{Tononi2008, Dehaene2011}, but ECC grounds this integration in physical field effects rather than purely neural computation.

The progression from dynamic core to IIT to ECC reveals an interesting theoretical evolution: the dynamic core hypothesis began with biological neural systems, IIT abstracted these principles into~\index{substrate independence!limitations of}substrate-independent information integration~\cite{Tononi2015, Tononi2021}, and ECC returns to physical implementation while maintaining mathematical rigor through its sheaf-theoretic framework and stress-energy tensor formalism.

These theoretical relationships help clarify how consciousness might have evolved~\cite{Goff2019, Thompson2018}. Rather than consciousness being either fundamental (panpsychism) or emerging suddenly through sufficient information integration (IIT), ECC suggests a gradual evolution of increasingly sophisticated patterns of energetic coherence in biological systems. This aligns with recent research in cellular cognition and basal awareness while explaining how more complex forms of consciousness emerged~\cite{Mathews2011, lyon2021reframing}.

\index{Integrated Information Theory (IIT)!relationship to panpsychism}\index{panpsychism!relationship to IIT}IIT's relationship to panpsychism emerges from its fundamental premise that consciousness is identical to integrated information~\cite{Tononi2016, Koch2019}. Since information integration exists to some degree in all physical systems, IIT leads naturally toward a panpsychist view where consciousness exists ubiquitously, though in varying degrees. This marks a crucial difference from ECC, which identifies consciousness with specific patterns of energetic coherence rather than information integration per se.

The ontological distinctions between ECC and IIT reflect broader debates about the relationship between physical implementation and mental phenomena~\cite{Nagel1979, Chalmers2018}.\index{energetic monism!as unified view}ECC's physicalist ontology positions energy flows as the fundamental constituents of both physical reality and consciousness, emphasizing direct causation through electromagnetic, chemical, and thermal dynamics. In contrast, IIT operates from an informational ontology where consciousness emerges from specific patterns of integrated information, quantified through $\Phi$~\cite{Oizumi2014, Tononi2021}.

These distinct ontological commitments shape how each theory approaches the relationship between brain and consciousness. ECC's emphasis on specific physical dynamics aligns with certain aspects of naturalistic approaches, while suggesting that consciousness requires particular forms of energetic coherence that cannot be reduced to abstract computation. IIT's more abstract framework raises questions about the relationship between information, causation, and physical implementation~\cite{Tononi2015, Koch2019}.

The theories also differ in their predictions about artificial consciousness~\cite{Koch2019, seth2024conscious}. ECC's insistence on specific physical implementation suggests strict constraints on what types of systems could potentially support consciousness, requiring particular forms of energetic coherence typically found in biological systems. IIT's more abstract framework allows for the possibility of conscious artificial systems, provided they achieve sufficient levels of integrated information~\cite{Tononi2021, butlin2023consciousnessartificialintelligenceinsights}.

\index{energetic monism!relationship to physical properties}\index{energetic monism!relationship to phenomenal properties}ECC's energetic monism suggests a unified view where energy serves as the foundational substance underlying both physical and phenomenal properties. IIT's framework, in contrast, suggests what might be termed an informational dual-aspect theory, where information possesses both physical and experiential aspects~\cite{Tononi2016, Koch2019}. This approach shares certain features with panpsychist perspectives while maintaining its unique emphasis on integrated information as the basis for consciousness~\cite{Mathews2011, Strawson2006}.

Through this comparative lens, we see how ECC and IIT represent distinct paradigms in consciousness research, each offering unique insights while maintaining different relationships to the physical implementation of conscious experience. Their contrasting approaches to physical substrates, integration, and phenomenology illuminate crucial questions about the nature of consciousness and its relationship to physical reality.
