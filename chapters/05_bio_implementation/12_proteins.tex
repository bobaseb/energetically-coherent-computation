\subsection{From Individual Proteins to Cellular Networks~\index{intercellular coupling}}

The relationship between protein states and neural energetics reveals fundamental principles about biological computation~\cite{Karplus2005, Zhou2008}. While individual proteins implement information processing through their complex conformational landscapes, consciousness emerges not from isolated molecular events but from coordinated activity across cellular networks~\cite{Hameroff2014, breakspear2005dynamics}.

This coordination requires mechanisms that extend beyond single proteins to enable direct coupling between cells, creating domains of shared states that can maintain coherence across neural tissues~\cite{Bennett2004, Sudhof2018}. The most fundamental of these intercellular coupling mechanisms are gap junctions—specialized protein channels that create direct cytoplasmic continuity between adjacent cells~\cite{Nagy2018, Giaume2010}.

Unlike the complex protein state transitions that mediate chemical synaptic transmission, gap junctions enable immediate electrical and metabolic coupling through physical connections between cellular interiors. These structures, composed of connexin proteins arranged in hexameric assemblies, represent a fundamentally different implementation of energetically coherent computation—one where information and energy flow directly between cells rather than through the elaborate molecular cascades of neurotransmitter signaling~\cite{Willecke2002, Pereda2014}.

As we transition from examining protein conformational states within individual cells to understanding how these molecular mechanisms coordinate activity across cellular networks, we must now explore how gap junctions create domains of direct cytoplasmic continuity that enable coherent states to propagate through neural tissues with minimal energetic cost and maximal efficiency.
