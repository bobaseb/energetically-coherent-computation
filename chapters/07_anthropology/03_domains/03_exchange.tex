\subsection{Exchange and Value Formation}

The anthropological analysis of~\index{exchange systems}exchange and~\index{value}value has evolved from early studies of the~\index{gift exchange}gift through substantivist-formalist debates to contemporary concerns with financialization and alternative economies.~\index{Energetically Coherent Computation (ECC)}ECC offers fresh insight into how~\index{value!emergence from coherence}value emerges from and remains grounded in patterns of~\index{energetic coherence}energetic coherence while enabling sophisticated cultural elaboration~\cite{mauss1925gift, gregory1982gifts}. Rather than choosing between materialist and symbolic approaches to value, the framework demonstrates how~\index{value!patterns of action}different forms of value emerge from specific configurations of social energy maintained through practice~\cite{mauss1925gift, gregory1982gifts}.

% GROUP 1: FOUNDATION CONCEPTS - GIFT EXCHANGE AND EMBEDDED ECONOMIES
Mauss's fundamental insight that~\index{gift exchange}gifts carry \textit{part of the soul} of the giver gains physical grounding through ECC~\cite{mauss1925gift}. Rather than treating this as metaphorical or mystical, we can understand how objects exchanged between people become imbued with specific patterns of~\index{energetic coherence}energetic coherence through their social circulation~\cite{miller2005materiality}. This explains both why certain objects acquire special value beyond their material properties and how they maintain this value across social transactions.

Polanyi's \textit{\index{economic activity!substantive versus formal approaches}substantive economics}~\cite{polanyi1944great} - how~\index{economic activity!cultural embeddedness}economic activity remains embedded in broader social relations - finds natural expression through ECC~\cite{tsing2015mushroom}. Different societies establish distinct but equally valid patterns of coherence for organizing production, distribution, and consumption. This explains both why certain economic forms prove remarkably stable within cultural contexts and why purely formal economic analysis often fails to capture the full complexity of~\index{exchange systems}exchange systems.

The framework particularly illuminates Graeber's theory of~\index{value!patterns of action}value as patterns of action~\cite{graeber2001toward}. Rather than treating value as either subjective preference or objective property, ECC demonstrates how~\index{value!emergence from coherence}value emerges from patterns of energetic coherence maintained through ongoing social practice~\cite{ingold2011being}. This helps explain both why certain forms of value prove remarkably stable across generations and how they remain open to transformation through changes in practice.

% GROUP 2: CULTURAL SYSTEMS OF EXCHANGE - KULA AND BEYOND
Consider kula exchange as analyzed by Malinowski~\cite{malinowski1922argonauts}. Through ECC, we can understand how~\index{inalienable possessions}kula valuables acquire and maintain their power not through arbitrary cultural assignment but through specific patterns of~\index{energetic coherence}energetic coherence established and maintained through ritual practice, social relationship, and physical circulation~\cite{strathern2004partial}. This explains both the remarkable stability of kula values and their resistance to reduction to either practical utility or symbolic meaning.

The investigation of what Zelizer terms \textit{\index{special monies}special monies}~\cite{zelizer1994social}. Different forms of currency and value-marking serve to establish and maintain specific patterns of coherence within social domains~\cite{bohannan1959impact, appadurai1986social}. This explains both why societies often maintain multiple, distinct forms of value and how these can resist reduction to purely economic calculation.

The framework particularly illuminates Weiner's \textit{\index{inalienable possessions}inalienable possessions}~\cite{weiner1992inalienable} - objects that resist complete commodification. Through ECC, we understand how certain items maintain patterns of~\index{energetic coherence}energetic coherence that transcend ordinary exchange value~\cite{nadasdy2007gift}. This helps explain both why some possessions prove especially resistant to marketization and how they maintain special status across generations.

% GROUP 3: VALUE CONVERSIONS AND TRANSFORMATIONS
This perspective proves especially valuable for understanding what Guyer identifies as \textit{\index{value conversion}scalar conversions}~\cite{guyer2004marginal} - how societies manage translations between different scales and forms of value. Rather than treating such conversions as either purely mathematical or arbitrary cultural constructions, ECC reveals how they emerge from and remain grounded in patterns of energetic coherence maintained through social practice~\cite{latour2005reassembling}. This explains both why certain conversion ratios prove remarkably stable and how they can shift under changing conditions.

Consider how societies maintain what Hart calls \textit{\index{value storage}memory banks}~\cite{hart2000memory} - systems for storing and transmitting value across time. Through ECC, we understand how different storage media - from ceremonial valuables to modern financial instruments - establish patterns of coherence that enable reliable value preservation while shaping social relationships~\cite{keane2003semiotics}. This explains both why certain forms of value storage prove especially effective and how they remain vulnerable to disruption.

The emergence of new forms of value in contemporary capitalism gains clarity here~\cite{appadurai1986social, ong2006neoliberalism}. Rather than treating financial derivatives or digital assets as either pure abstraction or simple commodities, ECC demonstrates how they establish novel patterns of coherence that enable new forms of value creation and circulation. This helps explain both their transformative power and their potential for generating systemic instability.

% GROUP 4: POWER, VIOLENCE AND VALUE RELATIONSHIPS
The relationship between~\index{value!power relations}value and violence takes on new significance too~\cite{graeber2001toward, foucault1980power}. As research has noted, systems of value often emerge from and remain backed by potential violence. ECC reveals how patterns of energetic coherence established through force can become stabilized into seemingly natural hierarchies of value. This helps explain both the persistence of inequitable value systems and their potential for transformation through collective action.

Taussig's analysis of commodity fetishism in South American mining communities gains particular clarity through ECC~\cite{taussig1980devil}. Rather than seeing such beliefs as either superstition or resistance, the framework demonstrates how they reflect sophisticated understanding of how value extraction disrupts established patterns of~\index{energetic coherence}energetic coherence~\cite{kohn2013how}. This explains both their persistence in the face of modernization and their power as critique of capitalist relations.

Consider how~\index{moral economies}moral economies operate, as analyzed by Thompson~\cite{thompson1971moral}. Rather than seeing these as either pure tradition or rational calculation, ECC reveals how communities establish coherent patterns of value that integrate economic necessity with social justice~\cite{tsing2015mushroom, wolf1999envisioning}. This explains both the remarkable stability of certain moral-economic arrangements and their capacity for mobilizing collective resistance when violated.

% GROUP 5: ALTERNATIVE CURRENCIES AND PAYMENT SYSTEMS
The framework also illuminates Maurer's work on~\index{exchange systems!alternative currencies}alternative currencies and payment systems~\cite{maurer2015how}. Different methods of payment - from shell money to digital wallets - represent distinct but equally valid patterns of coherence for managing social obligations and value transfer~\cite{appadurai1996modernity}. Rather than seeing modern financial technologies as simply more efficient than traditional payment forms, ECC reveals how each system establishes specific patterns of relationship while enabling different forms of social coordination.

Our exploration of exchange systems reveals a recurring pattern: value emerges not simply from material properties or arbitrary cultural assignment, but from who has the capacity to establish, maintain, and authenticate particular patterns of energetic coherence~\index{value!authentication and authority}. This observation directs our attention to the final core domain of anthropological inquiry: the relationship between knowledge and power~\index{knowledge systems!relationship to exchange}~\index{power relations}. While exchange systems organize how value circulates among social relations, knowledge systems determine which patterns of coherence count as authoritative and who has the right to establish them~\cite{foucault1980power, wolf1999envisioning}. The profound insight that power and knowledge are inseparable—famously articulated in Foucault's concept of power/knowledge~\index{power/knowledge}—gains physical specificity through ECC's framework~\cite{foucault1980power, latour1987science}. Just as value emerges from patterns of energetic coherence maintained through exchange practices, power operates through the capacity to shape what counts as legitimate knowledge~\index{knowledge production!authority relations}. In the next section, we will examine how different knowledge systems establish particular configurations of energetic coherence as authoritative~\index{knowledge systems!authority of}, whether through traditional expertise, scientific methodology, or bureaucratic procedure~\cite{strathern2004partial, nadasdy2003hunters}. This perspective reveals how Graeber's theory of value as "patterns of action"~\cite{graeber2001toward} extends directly into understanding how power operates through establishing which patterns of coherence are recognized as knowledge rather than mere belief or opinion.
